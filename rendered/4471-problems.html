How each report was placed

  1. Count each report once. The ticket export comes in pages that overlap, so some reports appear twice. One id, one report.
  2. Let the tenant decide, not the subject or the tag. Every tenant on file writes the day first. A report that came in through booking-v3’s own form is hint; every other report is parser (the rule in causes.ts). This is the desk’s own count, and the table uses it.
  3. Read what the report says. Almost every body describes a day and a month that swapped places: “booked 3 October, got an invite for 10 March”. A few describe something else: a date that moved by one day, or one meeting that showed two different days in two time zones. The desk already listed those in its notes as one-day moves. They are shift, a problem of their own. As the desk does with any report it reads differently from its cause, they stay counted where they are filed, with a footnote.

parser · the shared parser reads day-first dates month first

Every tenant writes dates day first. The shared parser reads every date month first, so a date like 7/4 becomes 4 July instead of 7 April, and the booking lands in another month. This is what #4471 describes, and the queued fix is for it: read dates in the tenant’s locale.

hint · booking-v3 prints an MM/DD hint to day-first tenants

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field for every tenant. A day-first tenant’s clients type the month first, as the hint tells them. The form then reads that field day first, the tenant’s way, and the date swaps before it ever reaches the shared parser. The parser fix does not touch this. The fix is in the form: print the hint in the tenant’s order (booking-v2 already prints DD/MM/YYYY), or replace the free-text field with a date picker.

shift · a date moves by one day with the time zone

These reports name no swap. Tuesday shows as Monday; a client in Toronto sees Monday while a client in Lisbon sees Tuesday for the same meeting; an invite arrives before the booking was made. That looks like a date-only value stored as a moment in one time zone and shown in another. Neither the parser fix nor the hint fix changes it.

Why the desk saw one

  • The subject cannot tell them apart. The desk merges reports whose subject lines match (decision 0001). All three problems arrive under the same subject lines (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Date is wrong”), so all three were merged into one ticket.
  • The intake tag is a guess. Intake tags from the reporter’s browser. The “tagged as” column shows how often that guess disagrees with the cause.
  • The page already showed it. The #4471 page’s own table lists hint as a second cause with “no ticket of its own”, and footnotes the one-day moves under parser. Its words still say one issue.
